## The visual result to deliver

An ordinary player walking through the fen should see an established wetland,
with vegetation growing together out of convincing ground, clear passages and
occasional taller groups that frame the surroundings. It should be recognisable
from the scene, without relying on its biome label or a close-up of one plant.
Carry the same kit into RF-06's existing dry lake-bank treatment where appropriate;
the fen is the primary art target, with one connected bank/home outlook as reuse.

The first pass delivered supported scattering but not this atmosphere. Its
small repeated upright clumps leave broad exposed terraces dominant. Plants,
ground and water read as separate layers; existing repeated tree silhouettes
still dominate. The functional checks do not establish visual success.

1. **Connected ground layer:** irregular low splayed sedge/leaf growth and damp
   litter should make rooted patches with broken edges, varied size and quiet
   gaps. Address the broad exposed ground between isolated dots. Avoid a uniform
   green carpet, coarse embossed bumps or a recolour of unchanged bare ground.
2. **Distinct middle layer:** compose taller, curved reed/rush groups with low
   spreading growth and a few grounded root/deadfall accents. Vary silhouette,
   orientation and group footprint; keep openings between masses. The purpose is
   visible layering at player height, not a specified instance-count increase.
3. **A place worth inhabiting:** frame a readable dry passage and an outlook with
   useful open ground. At the lake, connect dry ground to planted bank pockets
   while retaining visible shore, water and the home approach. Do not fill all
   open space or conceal enemies/resources to make one photograph look lush.
