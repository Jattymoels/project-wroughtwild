## The signature: broken material containing living light

Build each scar as three readable layers:

1. **Changed host:** compressed hide, lifted keratin plates, split bark, broken
   mineral bedding or weathered worked metal. Growth follows load and material
   structure; it does not resemble armour strapped to a normal animal.
2. **Dark fracture:** an irregular recessed edge, chipped or callused, that
   remains visible with emission disabled. Most fine branches end dark.
3. **Narrow luminous core:** light lies inside that recess, strengthens locally,
   travels through selected connected branches, then subsides. The host remains
   readable at the pulse peak. Avoid exposed lava cavities and floating tubes.

On the boar, begin under the shoulder shield, branch between a few major plates,
and let thinner scars continue into adjacent tough hide. Keep snout, eyes, leg
joints and most bristles legible. Do not illuminate every plate border. The
same motif follows grain on a root, bedding on a rock and a deliberately cut
channel on a crafted fitting. This is the visual link to the lore.

The owner's request brings forward clearly visible pulsing for the new study.
The existing ground material uses only 2.5% variation over 47 seconds
(`game/art/leyline_look.gd` and `leyline_fissure.gdshader`). That quiet treatment
is a baseline to compare, not proof that the requested pulse already exists.
The plan does not globally replace it before an in-engine review.

Separate **ambient life**, **actual work** and **immediate danger**. An ambient
scar can breathe without promising loot or an imminent attack. Source readiness,
extraction progress, machine requests and stored heat must read their real native
state; exhausted sources retain physical scars but lose the ready cue. Any
forming state must follow the selected source rules. Attack warnings retain
their existing clocks, footprint, contrast and priority over decorative light.

## Authoring and engine handoff

1. **Brief:** ordinary host, affected structure, influence, silhouette, attachment
   points, game role and views. Use the selected concept and actual failed views
   to correct ambiguity. Do not regenerate a liked silhouette just to add glow.
2. **TRELLIS source:** generate the major form and useful material suggestion.
   Preserve the exact input, seed, version and untouched export. Treat thin roots,
   leaves, mobile jaws, hidden anatomy and plate gaps as areas to inspect.
3. **Blender finishing:** repair visible form/attachments, establish deformation
   topology and UVs, then bake fine coat, bristle and fracture detail from the
   dense source. Keep silhouette-critical plates/roots as geometry. Nature
   clusters, modular ruins and precisely fitting fixtures can be assembled or
   modelled directly in Blender; not every part needs a new generated mesh.
4. **Scar data:** retain normal/roughness damage independently from the light.
   Author a core mask and a branch-position/phase mask so light can move along
   a chosen scar network. UV-attached masks must follow a skinned creature;
   world-space procedural cracks would slide when it walks. Start with one
   simple material shared by the pilot assets, not a general effects framework.
5. **Movement:** fit a rig and weight-bearing poses; adapt them to existing game
   clocks. Verify joints, feet, plate overlap, tusk roots and scars in motion.
   Existing moth geometry/rig is the preserved baseline, not another remake.
6. **Godot:** import the mesh, textures and rig, then explicitly bind the pulse
   material. Blender node animation is not assumed to survive a GLB export.
   Use a pause-aware cosmetic clock and stable per-instance phase offsets;
   gameplay events/state remain authoritative. Avoid a separate real-time light
   or script update for every crack. Check existing status/hit materials still work.
7. **Package:** editable source, runtime GLB, textures/masks, material settings,
   import recipe, LODs, preview clips, provenance and a short result/limitations
   record. Reopen the source and reimport the runtime artifact before adoption.

Godot 4.5 provides material emission, normal and roughness outputs; this supports
the proposed independent scar surface/light treatment. Its built-in shader
`TIME` does not stop on pause, hence the explicit cosmetic-clock requirement.
[Godot spatial shader reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.5/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/spatial_shader.html).
Bloom is an optional presentation aid, not the asset's only readable scar;
check the selected renderer and low-glow fallback during the engine proof.
[Godot environment reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.5/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html).

## ART-01 starting controls and acceptance

These began as **proposed comparison settings**. ART-01 now supplies the isolated
comparison and records its selected defaults; they are not installed game tuning. Store selected
values together in a named presentation resource with plain-language comments.

| Control | Initial comparison | What it controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pulse period | 2.5 seconds and 4 seconds, beside steady light | Whether the current feels alive without constant blinking. Use smooth rise/fall, not a hard flash. |
| Minimum core light | 25% and 50% of the chosen peak | Whether the scar remains readable between pulses. Establish peak brightness in the actual day/shade scene first. |
| Branch progression | Whole-scar breathing beside one travelling crest | Whether the asset conveys energy moving through material rather than a global material brightness slider. |
| Coverage | One primary scar system and sparse secondary branches | Keep the liked animal/material visible; do not trace every plate and fur clump. |
| Detail at distance | Hero view, ordinary encounter distance and distant silhouette | Decide what needs geometry, baked relief or a simpler LOD. Record exact review cameras. |

Use one consistent lighting/exposure setup across alternatives, plus daylight
and shade. Keep a low-motion/static comparison available for comfort review.
The task is done when the pulse is plainly visible, the dark scar survives with
emission off, it follows the moving body, and it remains distinct from existing
attack tells and status effects. Owner visual approval is separate from checks.

Record near/mid/far triangles, material surfaces, texture memory, draw calls,
skin cost and GPU/frame timings. Profile one hero and an existing approved dense
encounter; retain current enemy caps. Choose runtime budgets from that evidence
and visible LOD quality instead of applying arbitrary decimation to the raw
281k/296k meshes. No higher enemy cap or new performance requirement is selected
by this plan.
